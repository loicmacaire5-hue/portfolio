1

Reactivation of inactive customers

Role: Customer Retention Specialist | Skills: customer win-back, outbound calls, SMS and push, CRM, reporting

Situation

On online platforms, many registered customers stop buying after a while. Each inactive customer is lost revenue, and the business often does not know why they left.

Objective

Bring inactive customers back and learn the real reasons behind their inactivity.

Tools

B2B CRM, telephony, SMS and push messaging tools, daily call reports.

What I did

  • Identified customers who had been inactive for about one month.
  • Contacted them by phone, SMS and push notification, choosing the channel and time that matched their profile.
  • Asked a short survey question on the cause (payment problems, lost trust, lack of interest, offer problems, other).
  • Gave the right help or information during the call, or escalated technical problems.
  • Recorded every contact and answer in the CRM.
  • Reported the main causes and patterns so the platform could improve its offers and processes.

Outcome

Regular reactivation outreach within a monthly volume of 5,000+ calls and about 4,000 SMS, with about 3,250 calls completed successfully each month. The platforms received structured feedback on why customers stopped buying.

2

First-purchase follow-up for new customers

Role: Customer Retention Specialist | Skills: onboarding, first-purchase conversion, outbound calls, CRM

Situation

New customers often register but never make a first payment, so the welcome offer is never used and the acquisition cost is wasted.

Objective

Turn new sign-ups without a first payment into active customers during their first days.

Tools

B2B CRM, outbound calls, SMS follow-up.

What I did

  • Targeted new customers who had registered but not paid after about one week.
  • Called them to welcome them, explain the welcome offer clearly and answer their questions.
  • Helped customers blocked by a practical problem (payment method, steps to pay).
  • Noted the reasons for hesitation and passed recurring obstacles to the platform.

Outcome

A regular first-week follow-up routine across several African markets. New customers had a personal contact to explain the offer and the payment steps, and the platforms received feedback on what blocks new customers.

3

Customer support for payment issues

Role: Customer Support / Customer Success | Skills: problem solving, escalation, complaint handling, calm communication

Situation

During outbound calls, customers regularly raise urgent problems: payments not credited, withdrawals blocked or delayed. These customers are often frustrated, sometimes angry.

Objective

Resolve the issue quickly and correctly, keep the customer's trust and protect the relationship with the platform.

Tools

B2B CRM, internal escalation channel with technical teams, Zendesk live chat.

What I did

  • Listened without interrupting and let the customer explain the full situation.
  • Asked precise questions to find the cause: payment method, amount, limits, time of the transaction.
  • Solved the problem directly when it was within my scope.
  • Escalated to the technical service with a clear, complete description when it was not.
  • Followed up with the customer so the issue did not stay unanswered.
  • Stayed calm and professional when facing insults or strong frustration, without responding in kind.

Outcome

Payment issues were either solved on the call or passed to the technical team with a clear description. This work built my skills in objection handling, crisis handling and complaint resolution.

4

Customer segmentation and reporting

Role: Customer Retention Specialist | Skills: segmentation, data collection, reporting, insight

Situation

Platforms serve very different markets but often lack precise information about when and how to reach each type of customer.

Objective

Build useful customer groups and give the platforms clear, actionable information.

Tools

B2B CRM, call and activity dashboards, regular reports.

What I did

  • Collected each customer's preferred contact channel, best hours and region.
  • Identified and worked specific groups: top customers, discouraged customers, customers not using their offers.
  • Adapted my message to each group (for example reminders for unused offers, encouragement for discouraged customers).
  • Reported unused-offer cases and contact preferences to the platforms.

Outcome

Customer groups and contact preferences documented in the CRM, outreach adapted to each group, and regular reports giving the platforms practical information about customer behavior in their markets.
